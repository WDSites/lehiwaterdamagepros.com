Hardwood Floor Water Damage Repair in Lehi, UT

Wood floors react to water slowly, then all at once. Boards cup, crown, gap or buckle days after a leak. Hardwood floor water damage repair in Lehi, UT depends on how fast the floor is dried and how wet the subfloor became.

Wood Floors and Dry Air in Lehi

Wood floors move with moisture. The USDA Forest Products Laboratory says wood that loses moisture after installation shrinks and cracks open between boards, and that a significant increase in moisture content swells wood and can buckle a floor. That publication is from 1980, and its recommended 8 percent moisture content is for the central and eastern United States.

Utah winters are dry indoors with a furnace running. The EPA suggests indoor humidity below 60 percent and ideally between 30 and 50 percent, a range that also helps wood floors stay stable.

The EPA's lead renovation, repair and painting rule applies to paid work that disturbs painted surfaces in homes built before 1978, which matters for baseboards and trim around an older floor.

Sources: USDA Forest Products Laboratory (federal), EPA mold and moisture guide (federal), EPA lead renovation rule page (federal).

Cupping, Crowning and Buckling

What the board shape means
ShapeWhat happened
Cupping, edges higher than the centerThe bottom of the board took on more moisture than the top
Crowning, center higher than the edgesA cupped floor was sanded flat, then dried, or the top took on more moisture
Gaps between boardsThe wood dried and shrank
Buckling, boards lifting off the subfloorA major moisture event lifted the floor

Cupping often appears days after the water. A crew measures before deciding.

A floor that is cupped is not always ruined. Many floors flatten as they dry. The key is not to sand or refinish until the wood and the subfloor are dry.

Safety Steps: The First Hour After Water on Wood

  1. Stop the source of water.
  2. Mop or vacuum standing water with a wet-dry vacuum.
  3. Turn off power to the room if water reached outlets.
  4. Lift rugs and furniture and put foil or blocks under legs.
  5. Do not put heat on the floor, because rapid drying can crack boards.

Drying Hardwood Versus Replacing It

A crew drains the water, then measures the boards and the subfloor with a moisture meter. Special mats and panels pull moisture out through the top while dehumidifiers dry the air. This can take days, and the floor may be monitored for a week or more.

If the boards are glued to concrete, nailed over a wet pad or have cupped severely, replacement may be the more realistic answer. The crew shows you readings so the decision is based on numbers.

Insurance and Wood Floors

Policies often pay for damage from a sudden leak and not for gradual seepage. Ask whether your policy covers matching a floor that runs through several rooms.

Keep a record of the floor type, age and installation, plus photos before and after the leak.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Do not sand a wet or cupped floor
  • Do not seal moisture in with a new finish
  • Do not use heat guns or high heat to dry the boards
  • Do not leave a wet rug or mat sitting on wood
  • Do not install new wood over a wet subfloor
