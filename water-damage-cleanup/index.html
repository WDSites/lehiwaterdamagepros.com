The First 48 Hours Matter Most

Water damage cleanup is what happens between stopping the water and starting repairs. Pools come off the floor, soaked rugs and boxes come out, and the crew separates salvageable items from those that cannot be cleaned. Call as early as you can, at any hour, because the clock favors a fast start.

Same-day response is available when crews are free. Until someone arrives, keep walking in the area to a minimum, lift furniture off wet carpet, and open cabinet doors so air can reach the wall behind them.

What Cleanup Includes

Water and debris removal

Pumps, wet vacuums and extraction units remove standing water. Damaged boxes, rugs, pad and trim come out.

Sorting contents

Items are sorted into clean, restorable and discard piles. Photos are taken for your insurance file.

Sanitizing

Cleaners and, for Category 2 or 3 water, disinfectants are applied to surfaces that stay.

Deodorizing

Odor control starts after wet materials are removed, because masking smells over wet drywall does not last.

Cleanup does not include rebuilding. When the structure is clean and dry, the next phase is water damage repair. If the water is still coming in, the first call should be for emergency water removal.

A Washer Valve, a Toilet Line and a Dishwasher: Common Cleanup Cases

Appliance failures are a common reason for cleanup calls. A washing machine valve that breaks undetected can run for an hour and soak a laundry room, hallway carpet and the room below. A toilet supply line can spill into a bathroom and drop through the ceiling underneath.

Dishwashers and refrigerator ice-maker lines leak slowly, so the damage is under the floor before anyone notices. In all three, check beyond the visible wet spot. Water travels along floor joists and pools where you cannot see it.

Contents: What Gets Cleaned and What Gets Tossed

Hard items such as glass, metal and sealed plastic usually clean up well after clean water. Porous items such as upholstered furniture, mattresses and cardboard are harder to save, especially if the water was not clean. A crew sorts contents into keep, clean and discard piles so you can see the decision for each item.

Photograph discarded items before they go, and keep a list. Your insurer may ask for it, and the list is easy to build while things are still in front of you.

Odor After Cleanup

A smell that lingers after the surface is dry is a sign that moisture or residue remains. Odor can come from carpet pad, subfloor, a wall cavity or a drain. The fix is to find the source, not to mask the smell with fragrance.

If an odor returns within days, tell the crew. It often means a material that should have come out is still in place.

When Standing Water Is an Emergency

Treat water as urgent if it is still spreading, if it touches electrical equipment or if it came from a drain.

Shut the source

Close the main valve or the valve at the fixture.

Watch electricity

Leave the room if water reaches outlets or the panel, and call the utility or 911 first.

Do not wade in sewage

Water from a drain or toilet backup is contaminated. Keep people and pets out.

Save what you can

Move dry items to a dry room, and photograph everything that stays wet.

On insurance, sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Ask your insurer, and keep your photos and the written scope.

Cleanup Facts for Lehi Homes

Lehi City runs the culinary water, pressurized irrigation, sewer and storm drain systems through separate divisions of its Water Department. When water comes from a city-side line, the city can help with shut-off, so keep the department's number handy.

Irrigation water is not drinking water, so a crew handles an irrigation-line loss with more caution than a clean supply-line leak. Tell us which system the water came from when you call.
