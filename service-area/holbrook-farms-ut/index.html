Water Damage Restoration in Holbrook Farms, Lehi, UT

Holbrook Farms is a residential community in west Lehi built around single-family homes. Newer houses still get supply line failures, water heater leaks and appliance overflows. This page explains what we could verify about the area, what to do first and how drying works.

Common Causes in Single-Family Homes

Without documented local conditions, the useful guide is the source of the water. A washer hose can fail at its fitting. A dishwasher or refrigerator line can drip behind a cabinet. A water heater can leak from its tank or its relief pipe. Any of these can soak a floor and the wall base before it is noticed.

A sprinkler or irrigation line close to the foundation can also send water toward a wall. Finding the source is the first step, and it is often a plumber's job.

Safety Steps in the First Hour

  1. Close the shutoff valve for the leaking fixture or appliance, or the main valve if the fixture valve fails.
  2. If water is on the floor near outlets or appliances, do not stand in it to reach a breaker. Call an electrician.
  3. Move people and pets out of the wet room.
  4. Lift small valuables and rugs when it is safe.
  5. Take photos before you move or discard anything.

What Drying Involves

A crew removes standing water, then maps moisture behind baseboards and under cabinets with a meter and a thermal camera. Materials that cannot dry are cut out. Equipment then runs with daily readings until the structure matches a dry reference. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry reference for this work, named here as a reference and not as a credential.

Lehi's brochure notes that the city's responsibility for culinary water ends at the meter, so most leaks inside a home are handled by the owner, a plumber and the insurer.

Insurance Documentation

The Utah Insurance Department advises documenting damage with photos or video and keeping receipts, including extra living costs. It also says insurers must acknowledge a notice of loss within 15 days under Utah rules.

Whether a loss is covered depends on the cause and your policy. A sudden failure is often treated differently from a leak that went on for months.
