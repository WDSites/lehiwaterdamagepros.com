When Water Is Spreading in Draper

A storm that drops two inches of rain in an hour can flood a hillside basement faster than any pump set. Call at any hour. A Draper call can be met by a crew the same day if one is free, though we promise no arrival time.

Foothill Neighborhoods: Debris Flow and Cloudbursts

Draper's Hazard Mitigation annex records the risks. Post-fire mudslides hit the Corner Canyon area near Bear Mountain Drive in June 2009, and mudflows came down foothill drainages along Springdale Way in 2011. On April 22, 2023 a landslide slid two homes.

On August 3, 2023 more than two inches of rain fell in about an hour, with flooding, landslides and a road collapse. The city reported about 5.5 million dollars in damage. Steep eastern and southern foothills have moderate landslide susceptibility, and some areas near SunCrest are high.

If foothill runoff reaches your basement, the water is Category 3. See flood damage restoration, and call fast. The first call after an event like that goes to emergency water removal.

Which Water System Is Yours?

Draper has two water providers, and the city has a Utility Services App that tells you which one serves an address. After-hours emergency lines are listed on the city's water page.

Knowing which system a broken line belongs to saves time. Secondary water is typically on by April 15 and off around October 15, so a spring leak outdoors is more likely to be irrigation.

Historic Fort Street and Corner Canyon

Draper's historic sites include the Day Dairy Barn, Draper Park School and homes on Fort Street built between 1861 and 1898. Old masonry and old plaster take water slowly.

Corner Canyon Park and Ballard Arena sit on the foothill edge, and the SunCrest area has open space protected by easements. Homes near those edges are close to drainages.

Permits and Geotechnical Reports

Draper's Building Division takes permits through an online portal. A geotechnical report is required before a new-home permit, because of the foothill geology. For a repair, ask the division whether you need a permit.

If the damage came from a landslide or debris flow, ask about geotechnical review before rebuilding.
