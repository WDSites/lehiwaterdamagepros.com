Where Traverse Mountain Is

Lehi City's parks page lists Shadow Ridge Park at 3050 W. Traverse Mountain Blvd and Triumph Park at 1750 W. Traverse Terrace Dr., both with Lehi, UT 84043 addresses. Lehi's General Plan refers to a Traverse Mountain Area Plan, and a 2019 planning commission notice lists an amendment to it near 3000 West Traverse Mountain Boulevard.

The area is mixed. Lehi's plans place retail and office uses here along with housing, and a news report from 2025 names Vialetto as one residential area on Traverse Mountain. We describe the area by those landmarks and do not claim official boundaries.

What We Could and Could Not Verify

The City of Lehi provides culinary water, sewer and pressurized irrigation. Its brochure says the city maintains culinary water only up to the meter, so valves, fixtures and the water heater on your side are yours. That is why a leak inside the house is usually a call to a plumber and a restoration crew, not to the city.

In June 2025 KJZZ reported that the Vialetto area of Traverse Mountain was under watering restrictions while the city built a new pressurized irrigation system and reservoir, and that officials said residents had been using culinary water on lawns. That is a statement about irrigation, not about water damage.

We found no published flood or drainage history for Traverse Mountain. Lehi's 2014 storm drain master plan says the area was not modeled in that study and calls Dry Creek the only FEMA-studied channel in the city. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified.

Source types: Lehi City parks, water and sewer pages and utilities brochure, a Lehi planning notice, a KJZZ news report from 2025 and the Lehi storm drain master plan from 2014.

Water Problems That Fit a Hillside Community of Newer Homes

We cannot give a verified build-year or foundation breakdown for Traverse Mountain. Listing sites describe much of it as newer construction, but that is directory data and not an independent source. So we focus on causes that do not depend on the age of the house.

Typical examples include a washer hose, a dishwasher line, a water heater, a failed toilet supply or a pipe in a wall. Irrigation fittings near a foundation can also leak. Each starts with shutting off the right valve and then finding out how far the water traveled.

Safety Steps Before Anyone Starts Cleaning

  1. Shut off the water at the fixture valve or the main, only if you can reach it without standing in water.
  2. Stay out of standing water if outlets, cords or the panel could be wet. Have an electrician or the utility handle the power.
  3. Keep children and pets out of the wet area.
  4. Photograph the damage and the source when it is safe to do so.
  5. Do not try to dry sewage or contaminated water yourself with household fans or a vacuum. Call a professional.

How Drying Works After the Water Is Out

Pumps or wet vacuums remove the standing water first. A moisture meter and a thermal camera then show where water moved into walls, flooring and cabinets. Wet materials that cannot dry are removed, and air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference material.

The EPA says mold usually does not grow when damp items are dried within 24 to 48 hours, which is why speed matters. It does not promise that mold will never appear.

Insurance Documentation

Photos, a short written timeline and moisture readings can help you explain a loss to your insurer. The Utah Insurance Department says to call your insurer as soon as possible, keep receipts and wait for an inspection before permanent repairs.

Coverage depends on the cause and your policy. The department lists flooding and backed-up drains or sewers as generally not covered by standard homeowners policies, so ask your insurer what applies.
