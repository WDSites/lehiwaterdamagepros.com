Older Homes Near the Center of Town

Pleasant Grove was settled in September 1850 and incorporated in January 1855, when it was called Battlecreek. The city's history page describes strawberry fields that are now developed, and an original schoolhouse that is now a pioneer museum. Homes in town range from the original townsite to recent subdivisions.

Older homes tend to have older supply lines and drains, and those fail slowly. Water from a slow failure soaks framing and basement walls before anyone sees a stain.

Ask any crew to check every level of an older home, from the basement or crawl space to the attic, because water pools where you do not look. See water damage cleanup for what happens after the water is removed.

Secondary Water and the October Shutoff

The city's secondary water system supplies untreated irrigation water from ditches, streams and canals. It is not available west or south of State Street. For 2026, the city says it fills the system from mid-April and asks residents to wait until May 1 to water.

The system shuts off October 15, 2026 and drains for about a week. Valves stay open until Thanksgiving. A sprinkler line that is not blown out can split, and the water reaches a foundation wall in spring.

Battle Creek, Grove Creek and the Foothill Edge

Battle Creek runs in the canyon above town and gave Pleasant Grove its first name. Grove Creek Trail and Battle Creek Trail are on the city's parks page, with a trail to Battle Creek Falls. Creeks and foothill drainage raise the chance of runoff reaching east-side basements in heavy storms.

The city's storm water page describes curbs, collection boxes and underground pipes. We did not find a published history of floods in the city, so we make no claim about past events. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows whether a specific address is in a mapped zone.

Permits Before You Rebuild

The Building Division is part of Community Development and takes permits through an online portal. Its page says basements need a permit to finish, and it lists plan-check fees of $75 for basements. Hours are Monday to Thursday and Friday morning.

After a basement flood, ask the division whether your repair needs a permit before drywall goes back. Rules vary by scope.
