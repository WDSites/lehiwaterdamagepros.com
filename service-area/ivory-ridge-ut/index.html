Where Ivory Ridge Is

Lehi City's parks page lists Ivory Ridge Park at 601 West 3200 North, Lehi, UT 84043, with two pavilions, two playgrounds and a splash pad. That official listing is the strongest evidence that Ivory Ridge is a name used in Lehi.

Real estate sites describe it as an Ivory Homes master-planned community. We use the park address as our landmark and do not claim boundaries.

What We Could Not Verify

We found no published flood or drainage record for Ivory Ridge, and neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. We do not describe it as flood prone.

Listing sites suggest homes built mostly in the 2010s, but that is directory data we could not confirm independently. We also found nothing reliable on basements or slab foundations.

Confirmed citywide, Lehi's pressurized irrigation page says users turn off connections each year by October 15th, and the system stays active all year because it supplies fire hydrants. A line left charged after that date is a plausible source of a frozen fitting.

Source types: Lehi City parks page, Lehi pressurized irrigation page and real estate directories treated as unconfirmed.

Water Problems That Do Not Depend on Home Age

A failed toilet supply, a washer hose, a dripping ice-maker line and a water heater are common sources in any house. Frozen irrigation or hose-bib fittings are a winter source. Each can soak flooring, baseboards and lower drywall.

Water that has been in contact with only clean supply lines is easier to dry than water from a drain. If a drain or toilet is backing up, treat it as contaminated and skip the home drying steps.

Safety Steps for a Leak or Overflow

  1. Close the valve to the leaking fixture, or the main valve if needed.
  2. Keep out of standing water if outlets or cords may be wet.
  3. Do not use a household vacuum on water.
  4. If the water is sewage, keep people out and call a professional. Do not try to clean it yourself.
  5. Photograph the area and the source.

Extraction and Drying

Extraction comes first, because every gallon removed is one less for dehumidifiers to handle. Moisture mapping follows, then removal of wet materials that cannot dry, and then air movers and dehumidifiers with readings logged.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference behind these steps, and we cite it as a reference only. The EPA's guidance says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and that wall cavities are the hardest places to dry.

Insurance Documentation

Keep dated photos, a short timeline, receipts and any moisture readings you are given. Utah's insurance department says not to make permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected, and that the department cannot force payment or settle disputes.

A written scope can describe the work. It does not guarantee how an insurer will decide.
