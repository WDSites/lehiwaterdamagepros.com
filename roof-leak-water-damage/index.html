Snow, Wind and Roofs in Lehi

Lehi gets heavy winter snow and sudden spring melt, and both test roofs. The Lehi Free Press reported in March 2023 that Utah's snowpack had reached 23.2 inches of snow water equivalent, the highest in nearly 40 years, and the city began a flood mitigation plan that included advice to clear gutters and storm drains.

Much of Lehi is newer construction with low-slope sections, valleys and dormers where flashing can fail. Older roofs in the original town have had decades of freeze and thaw cycles on their seams and vents.

A University of Minnesota Extension guide, mirrored by the U.S. Department of Energy's Building America Solution Center, says ice dams form when parts of a roof are warmer than others, often because warm, moist air leaks into the attic. This is general cold-climate guidance, not a Utah study.

Sources: Lehi Free Press (local news, March 2023), University of Minnesota Extension and DOE Building America Solution Center (general guidance).

How Water Gets in Through a Roof

Most roof leaks are small. A cracked pipe boot, a lifted shingle, a nail that backed out or a failed seal around a chimney lets in a thin stream. That water runs down the underside of the sheathing until it meets a framing member and drops.

Wind-driven rain pushes water uphill under shingles, and a melting ice dam pushes meltwater under them. In both cases the roof deck can be soaked well before a drop reaches your ceiling.

Attic and Insulation Checks

Insulation is the first thing to soak. Wet batts lose most of their value and hold water against the ceiling board. Blown-in insulation clumps and is hard to dry in place.

A crew looks at the attic with a flashlight, a moisture meter and a thermal camera. Dark staining on sheathing, rusted nails and compressed insulation show where water has been entering over time.

Never walk on a roof covered with snow or ice to look for the leak. Use binoculars from the ground and call a roofer for the repair.

Safety Steps: Right Away

  1. Move people, pets and valuables out from under any bulge or drip in the ceiling.
  2. Place a bucket under the drip and poke a small hole in a bulging ceiling to drain it, standing to the side, only if it is safe.
  3. Turn off power at the breaker to rooms with wet light fixtures or outlets.
  4. Photograph the stain, the attic and the roof from the ground.
  5. Call a roofer for a temporary cover and call us to dry the interior.

A sagging ceiling can fall without warning. If you see a large bulge, stay out of the room.

Insurance and Roof Leaks

The Utah Insurance Department's home claims page lists wind and wind-driven rain among typical covered perils. It also says interior storm water damage is generally not covered when the roof and walls are undamaged. That distinction makes it important to document how the water entered.

Call your insurer, take dated photos and keep receipts for temporary repairs. Do not make permanent repairs before the insurer inspects.

Prevention and Common Mistakes

  • Clear gutters and downspouts before storms and before snowmelt
  • Have a roofer check flashing, vents and valleys after hail or high wind
  • Seal ceiling penetrations so warm air does not reach the attic
  • Do not paint over a stain until the cause is fixed and the board is dry
  • Do not ignore a stain that grows after a storm
