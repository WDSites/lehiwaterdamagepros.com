Freezing Nights and Lehi Houses

Lehi sits in a valley where cold air pools on clear winter nights, and many homes were built fast on slabs and in finished basements. Pipes that run along an exterior wall, through a garage ceiling or into a crawl space are the usual victims. A line that freezes and splits can sit quiet for hours.

Lehi City's pressurized irrigation page says owners turn their connections off each year by October 15th, and the system itself stays active all year because it supplies fire hydrants. A hose bib or sprinkler line left charged into November is a common place for ice to start.

Frost depth is a city rule that varies by town. Neighboring Spanish Fork's design criteria page lists a frost depth of 30 inches, and Lehi's own building page says current codes will be posted soon, so ask Lehi Building and Inspections what applies to your service line.

Sources: Lehi City pressurized irrigation page (city), Spanish Fork design criteria page (city, neighboring town), Lehi Building and Inspections page (city).

Why a Frozen Pipe Floods a House So Fast

Ice does not usually burst a pipe where the ice is. Pressure builds between the ice plug and a closed faucet, and the pipe splits at its weakest spot, often a few inches away. The crack stays plugged by ice until the temperature rises.

When the plug melts, the full pressure of the supply line is released through the split. A small crack can put out several gallons a minute, and a house can take on a lot of water before anyone wakes up or comes home.

Houses left empty over a holiday are the worst case. Nobody hears the first drip, and the water can run for a day or more through a ceiling, down a wall cavity and into a finished basement.

Safety Steps: The First Hour After a Freeze

  1. Find the main shutoff and close it. If the pipe has already thawed and is spraying, this is the step that stops the damage.
  2. Open the lowest faucet to drain the lines and relieve pressure.
  3. Leave power alone in any room where water is reaching outlets, switches or the panel, and call an electrician if you cannot reach the breaker safely.
  4. Do not use an open flame to thaw anything. A hair dryer or warm towels is safer for a pipe you can reach.
  5. Photograph the damage before you move belongings, then call a restoration crew and your insurer.

Insurance and Frozen Pipes

A sudden burst is usually treated differently from a slow, long-term leak, but the policy language decides. The Utah Insurance Department's home claims page says frozen pipe damage may not be covered if it was caused by negligence, such as not keeping the house adequately heated.

The same page says to call your insurer as soon as possible, document damage with photos or video, and hold off on permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Keep receipts for emergency steps, because the adjuster may ask for them.

Mistakes to Avoid and How to Prevent a Repeat

  • Do not turn the heat off in an empty house to save money
  • Do not leave hoses attached or sprinkler lines charged after the fall shutoff
  • Do not wait to see whether a thawed pipe is leaking, check along its whole length
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during hard freezes
  • Let a faucet drip on the coldest nights if a line has frozen before
