Basement Flood Cleanup Services in Lehi, UT: What To Expect

By the Lehi Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-25

Professional basement flood cleanup in Lehi means extracting the water, removing materials that cannot be saved, disinfecting what stays and drying the structure until readings match dry standards. Most of the cost and time are in the drying and the rebuild. This guide explains each step, what a flood does to a finished basement, and what Utah rules say about flood insurance.

Step One Is Safety

Before anyone starts, the basement has to be safe. Standing water near outlets, a furnace or a panel is an electrical risk, and sewage is a health risk. Stay out and call. A crew cuts power to wet circuits at the panel only if it is safe, and brings protective equipment.

If water came from a sewer or from outdoors, treat it as contaminated, because it is Category 3.

What the Crew Does

1

Extract

Pumps and extraction units remove standing water and pull water from carpet.

2

Remove

Carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that cannot be saved are cut out and bagged.

3

Disinfect

Cleaners and disinfectants are applied to surfaces that stay.

4

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings match dry standards.

Daily readings guide the work, and the crew leaves when the numbers say so, not at the end of a fixed number of days.

What a Flood Does to a Finished Basement

Basement materials after a flood
MaterialUsual outcome
Carpet and padReplaced after outside or sewage water
DrywallCut a few inches above the water line, often higher
InsulationReplaced if wet
Concrete slabCleaned and dried
Solid wood framingCleaned and dried
CabinetsSolid wood dried, particle board often replaced

Wicking carries moisture above the visible line, so crews cut higher.

Cleanup Is Not Waterproofing

Restoration repairs what the water damaged. It does not stop water from entering next time. If the same basement takes water every spring, call a drainage or waterproofing contractor as well.

We say so plainly. A dry basement with the cause unfixed is a temporary result.

How Long and How Much

Cleanup and drying usually take several days, and the rebuild adds more. The price depends on the water category, the square footage, the materials, the days of drying and the rebuild scope. We explain scope and price before work starts.

Lehi Flood Rules and Utah Flood Insurance

Chapter 12-D of Lehi's development code applies to FEMA special flood hazard areas inside the city. It requires a floodplain development permit for work there, and in A and AE zones it requires the lowest floor of a home, including a basement, to sit at least one foot above the base flood elevation.

The Utah Insurance Department states that a standard homeowners policy does not cover flood, and that flood policies usually have a 30-day waiting period. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows whether your address is in a mapped zone.

Source types: Lehi City development code Chapter 12-D, Utah Insurance Department flood FAQ.

Choosing a Basement Cleanup Company

The right company explains what it will do before it starts. Ask for a written scope that lists extraction, drying equipment, removal of damaged materials and a plan for readings. Ask what the price covers and what could change it.

A company that cannot explain its process or pushes you to sign immediately deserves another call to a second option.

Questions Worth Asking

  • Will you give me a written scope before work starts?
  • How will moisture be measured, and will I see the readings?
  • Who removes the damaged materials, and who disposes of them?
  • How are contents handled, and what do I need to move myself?
  • What happens if the readings do not reach dry on schedule?

Sump Pumps, Window Wells and Grading

A basement flood often traces back to a short list of causes. A sump pump that failed or lost power, a window well that filled with rain, a downspout that dumps water near the foundation, or soil that slopes toward the house are all common.

Cleanup removes the water that got in. Fixing the cause is a separate job for a plumber, landscaper or foundation contractor, and it is worth doing before the next storm.

Testing the Sump Pump Before It Rains

Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the pump start and empty it. Check that the discharge line runs well away from the house. A battery backup helps if power fails during a storm.

Test it a few times each year and after any heavy storm. A pump that sat unused for months can stick.

What Gets Removed From a Flooded Basement First

Wet carpet and pad are usually first, followed by the bottom section of drywall and insulation if the water stood against the wall. Baseboards and trim come off so the cavity can breathe. Anything that holds water and cannot dry quickly is a candidate for removal.

Removal can feel drastic, but it is faster and cheaper than letting wet materials sit and cause a second round of damage.

A Note on Stored Items

Cardboard boxes soak up water and fall apart. Move what you can to a dry place and photograph what you cannot save. Papers and photographs can sometimes be frozen to stop damage until you can deal with them.

Keep an inventory with a short description and rough value for each item.

Utilities in the Basement

Furnaces, water heaters and panels often live in a basement. If water reached any of them, leave the power off and call a licensed professional before restarting. Gas appliances need the same care.

A restoration crew can work around these units, but only a qualified trade can confirm they are safe to run.

What To Document

For your insurer

  • Photos of the water line and the damage before cleanup
  • The time you found the water and the cause, if known
  • A list of damaged belongings with age and value
  • The written scope and moisture readings from the crew
