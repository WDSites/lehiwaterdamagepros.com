Does Renters Insurance Cover Water Damage? Lehi, UT Guide

By the Lehi Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-25

Renters insurance usually covers your belongings when water damage is sudden and accidental, such as a burst pipe or an overflowing tub, but it does not cover the building itself. It often does not cover flood, and it often does not cover damage from a slow leak or neglect. This guide explains the split for Lehi tenants and what to do the day water appears.

What a Renters Policy Is Built To Cover

A renters policy has three parts that matter after a leak. Personal property coverage pays toward your belongings. Additional living expense coverage can help with a hotel if the unit cannot be lived in. Liability coverage helps if you cause damage to someone else's unit.

The building, the walls, the floors and the plumbing belong to the owner and are covered by the owner's policy. That is the first thing to understand, because tenants often assume the policy fixes the apartment.

Flood Is the Exclusion That Surprises People

The Utah Insurance Department states that a standard homeowners policy does not cover flood, and it describes separate flood coverage. Flood policies usually carry a 30-day waiting period, so a policy bought after the water arrives will not help.

Renters can buy flood coverage for contents. If you live in a ground-floor or basement unit near a canal, a window well or low ground, ask about it before spring runoff, not after.

The First Day: What a Tenant Should Do

Do these in order

  • Stop the water if you can safely reach a valve, and tell your landlord or property manager at once
  • Photograph the water, the source and your belongings before you move anything
  • Move valuables to a dry spot and keep receipts for any emergency purchase
  • Call your insurer to open a claim, and ask what it needs
  • Write down the time you found the water and who you told

Your landlord is responsible for the structure. Our water mitigation service can dry a unit when the owner or property manager authorizes it. Do not hire a crew for a rental without telling the landlord first.

What Landlords and Tenants Each Owe

Leases differ, and so do the steps. A landlord generally handles repairs to the building and the plumbing. A tenant handles reporting the problem quickly and protecting their own belongings.

If the cause was your own action, such as an overflowing tub, your liability coverage may help with the neighbor's damage. Read the lease and the policy for the exact terms.

Limits, Deductibles and Replacement Value

A renters policy has a total limit for personal property and a deductible you pay first. A small loss under the deductible may not be worth a claim at all. Read the declarations page for both numbers before you decide.

Policies also differ on whether they pay actual cash value or replacement cost. Actual cash value subtracts depreciation, so a six-year-old couch is paid at a fraction of the price of a new one. Replacement cost pays closer to what a new item costs.

Loss of Use: Where You Sleep While Repairs Happen

Many renters policies include loss of use or additional living expense coverage. It can help with hotel costs or extra housing expense if the unit cannot be lived in after a covered loss. Limits and time caps vary, so ask your insurer how yours works.

Keep every receipt, including food costs above your normal spending. A simple notes file with dates, amounts and the reason for each charge saves time when the adjuster asks.

Questions to Ask Your Insurer on the First Call

  • Was the water source sudden and accidental under my policy language?
  • What is my personal property limit, and what is my deductible?
  • Do I have actual cash value or replacement cost coverage?
  • Does my policy include loss of use, and how long does it last?
  • What documentation do you need, and how do I submit photos?

Write the answers down with the date and the name of the person you spoke to. Phone notes carry weight if a later conversation disagrees with the first one.

Where Restoration Fits in a Tenant Situation

In most leases the landlord controls the building and the tenant controls the contents. That means a restoration company hired by the owner dries walls and floors, while your own belongings may need separate attention. Tell the crew which items are yours, so they can be moved and photographed.

If you are not sure who should call a crew, call the landlord first and ask in writing. We can explain what our water damage cleanup work covers, and we never promise an insurance outcome.

Documenting Your Belongings

Insurers ask for a list of damaged items with age and value. A video walk-through of your unit, taken before any loss, makes the list easier. If you do not have one, photos of the damage and receipts or bank records help.
