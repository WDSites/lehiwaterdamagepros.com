How To Remove Black Mold From a Ceiling in Lehi, UT

By the Lehi Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-25

Removing black mold from a ceiling depends on what the ceiling is made of and how big the growth is. A small spot on a sealed, hard surface may be cleaned. Growth on painted drywall usually means cutting out the board, because mold roots into the paper. In every case, fix the moisture first, and keep spores from spreading to the rest of the house. This guide explains the steps and the limits of doing it yourself.

Fix the Water Above the Ceiling First

Mold on a ceiling is a symptom. The cause is water from above or steam from below. A roof leak, a plumbing leak, a failing vent or a bathroom with no ventilation can each do it. If you clean without fixing the cause, the spots return in weeks.

Check the attic or the floor above, and use a meter if you have one. Our water damage repair page covers rebuilding once the source is fixed.

The industry standard is ANSI/IICRC S520. You can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

If You Clean a Small Spot Yourself

  • Wear gloves, eye protection and a respirator rated for particles
  • Cover the floor and seal the doorway with plastic
  • Wet-wipe, never dry brush or use a fan
  • Bag wipes and debris and take them out right away
  • Do not paint over the spot until it is dry and the cause is fixed

Ladders and ceiling work add a fall risk. If you are not comfortable, call.

Protecting the Room Before You Start

Before touching a ceiling, protect the room. Cover the floor with plastic, close interior doors, and block off any return vent so spores do not travel through the house. Turn off the HVAC system if the area is close to a supply or return.

Open a window if possible and use a fan to push air outside. Wear gloves, eye protection, a long-sleeve shirt and an N95 respirator.

Cleaning a Small Spot, Step by Step

  1. Lightly mist the area with water or detergent solution to keep dust down.
  2. Wipe with a damp cloth and detergent, working from clean toward affected areas.
  3. Rinse the cloth often and bag used cloths before you leave the room.
  4. Dry the surface completely with a fan or dehumidifier.
  5. Check the area for a few weeks and watch for staining or recurrence.

Do not sand or scrape dry mold. Disturbing it dries it into dust and sends it into the air.

Priming and Painting Afterward

Prime the area with a stain-blocking primer once the surface is completely dry. Primer seals old stains and prepares the surface for paint. Choose a paint rated for kitchens and baths in humid rooms.

Painting over damp board or an active leak only hides the problem. If a stain bleeds through the primer, the board may still hold moisture.

What Professionals Add

A professional crew brings containment, air filtration, HEPA vacuums and the experience to find hidden moisture. They also decide if the board should be cut out rather than cleaned. The ANSI/IICRC S520 standard guides the approach as an industry reference.

For leaks that started above the ceiling, a crew can coordinate the drying and the repair so the area is dry before new board goes up.

Ladders, Reach and Safety

Ceiling work puts your face close to the surface, which is the worst position for breathing dust. Use a stable ladder, keep both feet planted, and avoid reaching beyond your balance. A helper to hold the ladder and move materials makes the job safer.

If the ceiling is high or the area is large, that is a good reason to hire a crew.

What the Attic Side Tells You

If you can safely look above the ceiling, check the insulation and the back of the board. Damp insulation, dark staining on the roof deck or a disconnected bath fan duct explain many ceiling problems. Those fixes come before cleaning.

Photographs of what you find help a roofer, an HVAC technician or an insurer understand the cause.

Texture, Popcorn and Older Ceilings

Textured ceilings trap dust and are harder to clean than flat paint. Scraping a textured ceiling releases material into the air, so do not do it without proper protection. Older texture products can contain materials that need testing before they are disturbed.

If the ceiling is textured and affected, ask a professional how to handle it.
