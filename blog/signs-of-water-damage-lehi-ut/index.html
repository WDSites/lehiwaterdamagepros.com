Signs of Water Damage in a Lehi, UT Home

By the Lehi Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-25

The earliest signs of water damage are usually a musty smell, a stain that comes back after painting, paint that bubbles in a band near the floor, or a water bill that rises with no change in your habits. Visible stains come late. This guide lists the signs in the order most people notice them, and explains which ones mean you should have moisture measured.

Signs You Can Smell and Hear

A musty or earthy smell in a closet, a bathroom or a finished basement is the first sign for many homeowners. It means a material has been damp long enough for microbes to grow. The smell often fades when a door is open and returns when it closes.

Sound matters too. A faint hiss behind a wall, a dripping sound with no visible drip and a water heater that runs more often than before can all point to a hidden leak.

Signs in Your Bills and Appliances

A water bill that rises with no new fixtures, guests or lawn work is one of the best early warnings. To test for a hidden leak, shut off every fixture, read your meter, wait an hour without using water and read it again. A moving dial means a leak somewhere between the meter and the house.

Appliances give clues as well. A washing machine hose bulge, a rusty water heater base and a refrigerator ice-maker line with a drip are all small failures that become large ones.

Signs After a Storm or Snowmelt

  • Water marks on basement walls or around window wells
  • A damp patch at a basement wall base after heavy rain
  • A smell that appears a few days after a storm
  • Stains on ceilings under a roof that was bare of snow after a melt
  • A sump pump that runs constantly or not at all

If any of these appear after a storm, treat the water as outside water, which is usually Category 3. See flood damage restoration for the steps.

What To Do When You Find a Sign

1

Mark it

Draw a pencil line around a stain and date it to see whether it grows.

2

Check the source

Look above and behind the sign. A supply line, a roof or a vent is often nearby.

3

Measure moisture

A meter in a technician hands tells you more than a guess.

4

Dry and repair

Fix the source, dry the structure, then repair the finish.

A Room-by-Room Walk Through

Walk your house once with a flashlight and a notepad. Start under every sink and look for staining, swelling or a rusted trap. Check the base of the toilet for soft flooring, the washing machine hoses for bulges, and the water heater for drips or rust on the pan.

In a basement, look at the bottom of walls, the corners and the area around the sump pit or floor drain. Look up as well, since a stain under a bathroom often points to a leak from above.

The Water Meter Test

A simple test can tell you if there is a hidden leak on the supply side. Turn off every fixture and appliance in the house, then look at your water meter. If the leak indicator or the dial is still moving, water is going somewhere.

Take a photo of the reading, wait an hour without using water, and look again. A change points to a leak between the meter and the fixtures. Call a plumber with that photo in hand.

Why Small Signs Deserve Attention

Water that leaks slowly can run for weeks before a stain appears. During that time it can soak framing, insulation and subfloor, and it gives mold what it needs to grow. Repair costs usually rise the longer the water goes unnoticed.

That is why a musty smell, a warped baseboard or a stain that reappears after repainting deserves a closer look rather than another coat of paint.
