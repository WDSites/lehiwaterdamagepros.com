Carpet Water Damage in Lehi, UT: Dry It or Replace It?

By the Lehi Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-25

Wet carpet can sometimes be saved if the water was clean and the drying started within a day. The pad underneath rarely survives, and carpet from sewage or flood water usually does not. The deciding factors are the water category, how long the carpet stayed wet and whether the subfloor is wet too. This guide helps you decide.

Speed is the lever you control. The sooner water is extracted, the more of the carpet can be saved.

Why the Pad Is the Problem

Carpet pad acts like a sponge. It holds water against the subfloor long after the carpet surface feels dry. Pad is also cheap compared with the cost of drying it, so crews usually replace it.

If the pad is removed, the carpet can be lifted, racked and dried on a stretched frame while air movers push air under it. Then the carpet goes back on a new pad.

What To Do While You Wait

  • Shut off the water source and keep traffic off the carpet
  • Lift furniture or place foil or wood blocks under the legs
  • Run fans to move air, if the water was clean and the area is safe
  • Photograph the wet area before anything moves
  • Call a crew if the carpet stays wet after a day

If the water is still standing, call for emergency water removal first. Extraction pulls more water than any fan.

Subfloor and Tack Strip Checks

Under carpet and pad lies a subfloor, often plywood or concrete. Plywood can swell and delaminate, and tack strips and baseboards can hold moisture. A meter checks them.

A slab basement floor dries differently from wood framing, and a technician sets a plan for each.

How Water Category Changes the Answer

Clean water from a supply line is the best case, and carpet can often be dried in place if crews respond quickly. Water from a washing machine drain or a dishwasher is gray water and carries more contamination. Sewage and floodwater from outside are the worst case, and carpet exposed to them is normally removed.

These categories come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which crews use as an industry reference. A crew should tell you the category in writing before deciding to save or replace.

Drying Carpet in Place: What It Takes

In-place drying can work when the water was clean and the carpet is not delaminating. Crews lift the carpet, remove or replace the pad, dry the subfloor and set air movers to push air under the carpet. A dehumidifier pulls moisture out of the air so it does not return to the materials.

The carpet is checked daily and the readings are logged. If the backing starts to separate or the smell lingers, the plan changes to replacement.

What to Move Before the Crew Arrives

  • Small furniture, so the carpet and floor can be reached
  • Rugs, books and papers on the floor
  • Electronics, kept away from any wet area
  • Foil or plastic under furniture legs to prevent stains

Carpet on Concrete in a Lehi Basement

Concrete slabs hold water and release it slowly, so carpet over a slab can stay damp long after the surface feels dry. A moisture meter or a plastic sheet test can show whether the slab is still releasing water.

If a basement slab keeps feeding the carpet, the carpet will not stay dry. That is a moisture source problem, not a cleaning problem, and it needs a different fix.

Area Rugs, Padding and Stairs

Wool and natural fiber rugs can hold water and shrink, so they are often moved out for professional cleaning and drying. Synthetic rugs dry faster, but the floor beneath them still needs checking. Lift any rug on a wet floor and look underneath for trapped moisture.

Stairs are tricky because water runs into the carpet tack strips and the stringers. Crews check the treads and risers when carpet on a staircase gets wet.

Replacement Choices After a Loss

If carpet has to go, think about what replaces it. Carpet over a slab holds moisture, while luxury vinyl plank, tile or sealed concrete handle future spills better. In a basement, the flooring you choose can decide how a future leak plays out.

Match thickness to the neighboring room so thresholds line up. Ask the installer to confirm the subfloor is dry before anything goes down.

A Short Story About Timing

A wet carpet that is dried within a day or two has a much better chance of being saved than one that sits for a week. Delay lets contaminants spread through the pad and invites odor. That is why the first calls matter more than the final choices.

If you cannot reach a crew right away, run fans, lift furniture and extract what you can with a wet vacuum rated for liquids.

Pets, Kids and a Wet Carpet

Keep children and pets off a wet carpet, since wet areas can hide hazards and carry contamination from the source. Pet urine and spills add their own odors, so tell the crew about any earlier accidents.

A short phone call before work starts lets the crew bring the right products and plan for the room.
