What Does Mold Look Like? A Lehi, UT Homeowner Guide

By the Lehi Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-25

Mold can look black, green, white, gray or orange, and it can be fuzzy, flat, slimy or powdery. It often shows as spots that spread on a damp surface, with a musty smell. Color does not tell you the type or how serious it is. This guide shows what to look for, what can be mistaken for it and when to call.

Common Looks of Mold in a House

Appearance and where it shows
AppearanceWhere it often appears
Black or dark green spotsBathroom ceilings, window frames, shower corners
White, fuzzy or powdery patchesBasement walls, stored boxes, wood
Green or blue-green growthWet drywall, wallpaper, food
Gray or brown patchesWood and fabric
Pink or orange filmsShower stalls and drains, often bacteria rather than mold

These are descriptions, not diagnoses.

What It Can Be Mistaken For

Dirt and soot can look like mold, and so can stains from old water. Efflorescence, a white mineral crust on concrete and brick, looks like white mold and is not. A quick check is to wipe a spot with a damp cloth. Dirt wipes off cleanly and mold often smears.

Do not sniff closely or disturb a large patch. Photograph it and leave it alone.

Where Mold Hides

  • Behind baseboards and under sinks
  • Inside wall cavities next to showers and tubs
  • Under carpet and pad after a wet event
  • On the back of drywall in a basement
  • In attic insulation near roof leaks
  • Around windows where condensation collects

You may smell it before you see it. A musty smell in a closed room is a prompt to check for moisture.

Lehi Context

Lehi's Main Street Historic District holds brick buildings from the 1890s through the 1920s with limestone foundations, according to the National Register record. Masonry that old can hold moisture and show white crusts that are not mold.

Newer homes have the opposite risk, tight construction that traps humidity. Either way, check moisture before deciding what you are looking at.

Color Is Not a Reliable Guide

Mold can look black, green, white, gray, brown or even orange. The same species can change color as it ages, and different species can look alike. Judging by color alone leads to confident guesses that may be wrong.

That is why the useful question is not what color it is, but whether there is a moisture source and whether the material is damaged.

Texture and Pattern Clues

  • Fuzzy or velvety patches that sit on a surface
  • Powdery spots that smear when touched
  • Spots that cluster in a ring or spread outward
  • Staining that follows the grain of wood
  • Peeling or bubbling paint over a damp area

These clues help you describe what you see when you call for advice. They do not replace a proper inspection.

Photographing What You Find

Take a wide photo to show the room and a close photo to show the surface. Add a coin or ruler for scale. Note the date, the room and any recent leaks or high humidity.

A good photo set helps an inspector, an insurer or a landlord understand the situation before anyone arrives.

Where to Look When You Cannot See It

Hidden growth can sit behind furniture, under carpet edges, inside cabinets and above ceiling tiles. A musty smell with no visible source is a clue to look in those places. A moisture meter and a flashlight make the search easier.

If you find damp material, the cause matters as much as the growth. Water has to come from somewhere.

Mold, Mildew and Dirt: Telling Them Apart

Mildew is a common word for mold growing flat on a surface, often white or gray. Dirt and soot sit on top of paint and usually wipe off without leaving a stain. Efflorescence is a white crystalline deposit that comes from minerals in masonry and does not grow.

A simple test is to wipe a small spot with a damp cloth. Dirt and mineral deposits often lift or dissolve differently from growth, though this does not replace a proper inspection.

Common Household Lookalikes

  • Soap scum and mineral buildup near fixtures
  • Smoke or candle soot on walls and ceilings
  • Dust collecting around vents and registers
  • Old water stains with no growth
  • Dark grout lines that need a deep clean

If a spot returns after cleaning, or if the area is damp, treat it as possible mold and look for the water source.

Why Documentation Beats Guessing

Photos with dates build a record. If a spot grows over days or returns after cleaning, the pattern tells you more than any single look. Share the record with a professional, an insurer or a landlord.

A written record also helps if you ever sell or rent the property, because it shows when you noticed and what you did.

A Simple Home Inspection Routine

Once a season, check under sinks, around windows, behind furniture on exterior walls and in the corners of ceilings. Bring a flashlight and look at angles, since mold hides in shadows. Note anything damp or stained and take a photo.

Small, regular checks catch problems before they become expensive.

Smell matters too. A musty odor with no visible source often means growth in a hidden place, such as behind a wall or under flooring.

Follow the smell to the strongest spot and look for dampness there.
