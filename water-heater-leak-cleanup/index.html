Water Heaters in Lehi Homes

Lehi had about 95,000 residents in July 2025 by Census Bureau estimates, and many homes are newer builds with the water heater in a utility closet, garage or finished basement. Closets and basement corners hide a slow leak for months.

The State of Utah lists the editions of the International Plumbing, Mechanical and Residential codes it adopts in Utah Code 15A-2-103. Lehi's Building and Inspections page says current codes will be posted soon, so ask them which edition applies to a replacement heater.

The EPA's WaterSense home maintenance page says to inspect the water heater regularly for leaks, aged gaskets, loose connections and corrosion, to flush it yearly and to test the temperature and pressure valve at least once a year.

Sources: U.S. Census Bureau QuickFacts, Utah Code 15A-2-103 (state statute), Lehi Building and Inspections page (city), EPA WaterSense.

A Water Heater Leak Versus a Flood

A drip at the fitting is a small leak, and a failed tank is a flood. The first can sit in a pan for weeks. The second can put dozens of gallons on the floor in minutes, then keep running until the cold supply is closed.

Both leave the same questions. How far did the water spread, did it reach drywall, and is the floor under it still holding moisture?

Signs Your Water Heater Is Leaking

Reading the clues
What you seeWhat it can mean
Water in the drain panA fitting, valve or tank is leaking
Rust streaks on the tank or the floorLong-term corrosion or a slow drip
Dripping from the relief valve pipePressure or temperature is too high
A warped baseboard near the heaterWater has been wicking into the wall
Lukewarm water and a wet floorThe tank may have failed

Read the water meter with every fixture off, wait two hours and read it again. A change points to a leak somewhere. The EPA suggests this check for hidden leaks.

Safety Steps: The First Hour With a Leaking Heater

  1. Turn off the cold water supply to the heater. The valve is usually on the pipe above the tank.
  2. Shut off power at the breaker for an electric heater, or set a gas heater to pilot and close the gas valve if you are comfortable doing so.
  3. Stay out of standing water if the water could be touching outlets or cords.
  4. Do not touch the relief valve pipe, because water from it can be scalding.
  5. Take photos, then call us for the water and a plumber for the heater.

Cleanup After a Tank Leak

1

Extract

A wet vacuum or pump pulls standing water from the floor so drying can start.

2

Map moisture

A meter and a thermal camera show how far water traveled into walls, cabinets and flooring.

3

Remove

Baseboard, wet insulation and board that will not dry are removed so the cavity can air out.

4

Dry and log

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference material.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published reference behind these steps, and we cite it as a reference only. The heater itself is a plumber's job, and a new unit should not be installed on a wet floor.

What Changes the Cost

  • How long water ran and how many gallons reached the floor
  • Floor type, such as concrete, vinyl, tile or carpet
  • Whether water reached drywall or neighboring rooms
  • Whether the closet or basement needs open air for drying
  • Days of equipment and the number of visits for readings

Insurance and a Failed Water Heater

Insurers often separate a sudden tank failure from a long, slow leak. The first may fall under the sudden and accidental language in a homeowner policy, and the second may be treated as maintenance. Policies vary, so read yours or ask your insurer.

The Utah Insurance Department says to call your insurer promptly, document the damage, and keep receipts for temporary repairs. Save the failed tank or its label if the adjuster wants to see it.

Prevention and Mistakes to Avoid

  • Put a drain pan with a drain line under the heater if there is none
  • Test the relief valve once a year as the EPA suggests
  • Check the tank base for rust or moisture every few months
  • Do not store boxes against the tank where a leak can hide
  • Do not ignore a slow drip because it only fills a cup
