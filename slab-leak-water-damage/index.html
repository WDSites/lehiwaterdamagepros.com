Emergency Slab Leak Water Damage in Lehi, UT

A slab leak is a leaking water line under a concrete floor. It is less common in Lehi than basement or plumbing leaks, because many homes here have basements, but garages, additions and some slab homes do exist. This page covers the real local cases and when to look elsewhere.

How Common Is a Slab Leak in Lehi?

Honestly, we could not find a source for how many Lehi homes are built on slabs rather than basements, and the Census data does not break out foundation types. Many homes here have basements, so for most callers the cause is a pipe, an appliance or a storm, and those pages come first.

Slab-on-grade sections still exist in garages, additions, some townhomes and older ranch homes. The Utah Geological Survey notes that shallow groundwater and saturated soils are features of many Wasatch Front valleys, which can add to the movement and moisture around a foundation.

The EPA's WaterSense program describes a simple check for a hidden leak. Read the water meter with every fixture off, then read it again two hours later. If the number changed, water is leaving the system, and a slab line is one of the possible places.

Sources: Utah Geological Survey Public Information Series 28 and 100 (state agency), EPA WaterSense home maintenance page (federal).

Signs of a Leak Under the Slab

What a slab leak can look like
SignWhat it can mean
A warm spot on a floorA hot line under the slab is leaking
The sound of running water with taps offPressurized water is escaping somewhere
A water meter that moves with everything offA line has a leak, inside or outside
Damp or lifting flooringWater is coming up through the slab
Baseboard that stains from the bottomWater is wicking into the wall from the floor

These signs are clues. A plumber with leak detection equipment confirms the location.

Safety Steps: The First Hour With a Suspected Slab Leak

  1. Shut off the main water valve and see whether the meter stops.
  2. Keep power off in any room where water is on the floor or near outlets.
  3. Do not break into the slab yourself.
  4. Take photos of damp areas, stains and any meter readings.
  5. Call a plumber with leak detection tools and call us for water removal and drying.

If the leak is on the hot side, hot water can be a scald risk. Stay clear of wet floors that feel warm.

How Slab Water Is Dried

Concrete holds water and gives it up slowly, so a slab can stay damp long after the surface looks dry. Flooring over a slab, such as laminate, hardwood or carpet, traps that moisture and can swell or grow mold.

A crew lifts the flooring where needed, removes wet pad, and sets air movers and dehumidifiers. Meter readings on the slab and nearby walls are logged until they match dry reference material.

For concrete and flooring, the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the reference for drying goals. The slab leak itself is repaired by a plumber, and drying should not be finished until the line has been fixed.

Insurance and Slab Leaks

Policies treat slab leaks differently. Some cover the resulting water damage but not the cost to reach the pipe, and some exclude damage from leaks that go on for a long time. Ask your insurer which applies.

Keep the plumber's report, photos and receipts. A written location of the leak helps the adjuster understand the cause.

Mistakes to Avoid and Prevention

  • Do not install new flooring over a damp slab
  • Do not ignore a meter that keeps moving
  • Do not run a hot water line that you suspect is leaking for long
  • Test the main shutoff yearly so it works when needed
  • Keep an eye on water bills for sudden jumps
